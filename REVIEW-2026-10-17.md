# Peer review of the AFLW model, 17 October 2026

A full pass over the pipeline (`run-round.js` and every script it calls), looking for holes. Each suspicion was tested on data before anything changed. Only fixes with evidence (or a clear logic error) went live. Backups from before the review are in `backups/before-review-2026-10-17/`.

## Fixed

| # | Hole | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The weather adjustment double-counted.** Its constants (−0.517 pts per km/h of wind, −9.5 for rain) were fitted on raw totals against the season average, in-sample, before the ratings existed. The ratings already know a ground's usual conditions. | On 2024–26 with archived match weather, the old adjustment made totals **worse**: MAE 16.79 vs 16.51 with none. | `conditions-model.js` refits the adjustment on the **model's own total error**, walk-forward, every week. Each term is centred on its mean, so the average total doesn't move. |
| 2 | **Kickoff time was missing**, and it is the largest pattern left in totals. | Residual by local kickoff (2023–26): +7.8 before 1pm, +3.1 1–3pm, +0.8 3–5pm, −4.3 6–7pm, −6.4 after 7pm. The night gap grew each season (−5.0, −5.7, −7.1, −8.1). Night games are also calmer (12 vs 16 km/h), so the old wind term pushed night totals *up*. | Kickoff hour is a term in the conditions model: −3.3 pts per 3 hours later (SE 1.0), with wind −0.54 per km/h (SE 0.11) and rain −11.9 (SE 3.1). Walk-forward RMSE 22.19 vs 22.30 (2024–26) and level on 2022–26. The night residual falls from −6.6 to −5.0. Not all of it is captured, because the effect has grown. Revisit with recency weighting after the season. |
| 3 | **Rain ignored its probability.** The full −9.5 step switched on or off from the deterministic forecast, so 0 mm at a 35% chance counted as dry. | 2026 rounds 3–10: the forecast flagged 3 of the 8 wet games and had no false alarms. It under-calls rain. | Rain enters as the forecast **chance** of rain times the rain effect (an expected value). Forecast wind is shrunk 20% toward the mean, because it regresses that much onto the archived value (slope 0.80, corr 0.85). |
| 4 | **Draws were read the wrong way.** The win chance is an *expected score* (a draw counts as half): that's what Elo predicts and what every backtest grades. The margin was solved from P(margin > 0.5) = p, which treats it as an outright-win chance. Every margin sat 0.5 pts toward home, and the away head-to-head price left out its half of the draw. | Walk-forward 2024–26 with the expected-score convention: Brier 0.1663 vs 0.1664, log loss 0.5073 vs 0.5076. Also better on 2019–26. A small but consistent bias. | Margin solved from P(margin > 0) = p (`stats.js`, `backtest-model.js`, `predict-round.js`, `betting-odds-model.js`). Full-game head to head is now **two-way with dead-heat rules**, exactly like Australian bookmakers' AFL markets. The three-way price, with the draw on its own, is kept in `result3`. |
| 5 | **Elo never moved on a draw**: log(0 + 1) = 0, even when a heavy favourite was held. | 12 draws since 2017. Brier 0.18760 → 0.18758. | A draw counts like a one-point game, with no winner for the autocorrelation term. |
| 6 | **The H1/Q1 total spreads were frozen August constants** (12.87 / 9.18). | The modern era's are now 13.47 / 9.37: too narrow as scoring rose. | Re-measured weekly by `backtest-model.js` (`model_params.halves`). |
| 7 | **Finals would have been labelled "Round 13–16".** | The 2026 rounds list has rounds 13–16 named "Qualifying & Elimination Finals", "Semi Finals", "Preliminary Finals" and "Grand Final". | `fetch-upcoming-round.js` carries the round name. `update-fairline.js` writes it to the board's `board-meta` block and title, and `run-round.js` reads the round on the board from that block. The old "Round N" text swap also matched "Round 10" when replacing "Round 1". |
| 8 | Derby note read "within 0/0km". | Cosmetic. | Now: "both teams are based within 100km of the venue, so no home-ground advantage". |

## Checked and left alone (no hole, or not worth the change)

- **"Totals run 3 pts high"** (the 14 Oct finding). This is noise: live 2026 bias +2.8, SE 3.35, almost all from round 8, where the average total was 64. Walk-forward bias by season: −3.6, +2.3, −1.0, +1.0 (2023–26).
- **Team news counted twice?** Injured players are missed by the lineup measure *and* by the career-games adjustment, whose slope was fitted without the lineup term. Fitting both jointly was **worse** out of sample (Brier 0.1677 vs 0.1664 for 2024–26, and 0.1711 vs 0.1705 for 2019–26). Kept as is.
- **Weighting of the two totals** (scoring model vs ridge): 50/50 is near-optimal. 40/60 is 0.006 MAE better in hindsight, and picking the weight on earlier seasons does worse.
- **Blend spread from modern seasons only:** no gain.
- **Data quality:** no duplicate matches, 18 canonical teams, every match has a venue and four quarters, and the 12 draws are all real.
- **Calibration** (deciles, 2019–26 walk-forward): χ² 9.2 (pass < 18.3). The lowest decile still under-predicts heavy home underdogs (7% predicted vs 12% observed). This is the existing watch item, not new.
- **The scoring shots share in the ridge rating** (`PPS` uses all seasons, a negligible look-ahead) and the first-score coefficients (fitted on 726 games): fine.

## Still open

- The night/kickoff effect is growing. A recency-weighted conditions fit is the next test once 2026 is complete.
- The lineup measure is the model's biggest single swing (e.g. Carlton v Bulldogs, round 10: −6.6 pts, against a market 8 pts the other way). It has been validated walk-forward, but games where it disagrees sharply with the market are the ones to watch in the Record tab.
- No proven edge: on 45 closing prices the model is level with the market (log loss 0.500 vs 0.499). Line and total bets are ahead (+5.1u on 10 and +3.9u on 11), but luck does that 3–7% of the time.

## App changes the same day (Fairline)

- **16 bookmakers.** EdgeFinder's new read-only `/api/comp` (service binding `EDGEFINDER`) adds TAB, Neds, PointsBet, Betr, Dabble, Unibet, TABtouch, PlayUp, BetRight, EliteBet, Palmerbet, BetDeluxe and BetAus. These sit alongside Fairline's own live Sportsbet, Betfair and Ladbrokes fetches. Every price is scored at its own line: EV = P(win) × price + P(push) − 1, dead heat on head to head, Betfair after 5% commission.
- **Sportsbook-style UI** (`public/app.js`, `public/app.css`):
  - Match cards with Win/Line/Total buttons, the best bookmaker price under each fair price, and value marked green (2%+) or amber (10%+: check the news first).
  - A comparison table per game.
  - A Best value tab, which hides long shots by default.
  - The prediction's reasons (lineups, team news, conditions) shown on each game.
  - The Record tab is kept.
- `/api/market-beaters` is cached in the Worker's cache instead of KV, which keeps KV writes for the price history.

### App peer review: 7.4 → 8.3 out of 10

An independent reviewer checked the live site at 390, 360 and 1280px, in light and dark, with the clock moved past the bounces. It recomputed every EV from the API and found no mismatches.

**Round 1 (7.4).** Fixes:
- Started games are no longer compared, on either the server or the page.
- The sticky tabs sit under the real header height.
- Margin bands wrap on phones.
- Value counts only 2–10% edges, with 10%+ as a separate amber "check first" section. EVs are capped at +50%+, and long shots are never coloured.
- Betfair counts only with a spread of 3 points or less and $50 or more waiting. Otherwise it's shown greyed as "thin".
- `?refresh=1` is throttled to 45 seconds.
- The hero is compact, with the notes in "Why this price".
- The best cell is always marked, and value rows have a link out to the bookmaker.
- Ladbrokes specials are turned to Fairline's home side.
- A failed source shows a red or amber dot, a callout naming it, and a Retry button.
- Desktop has a two-column grid with navigation in the header, there is one toggle style, and colours have fallbacks for older iPhones.

**Round 2 (8.3, passes).** Leftovers fixed after:
- **Placeholder lines and totals** are shown greyed as "unconfirmed" and never count as best or value. Examples: PlayUp's 100.5 @ 1.87/1.87 on several games; a lone number more than 15 points from the model; a number more than 6 (line) or 8 (total) points from the other books' median.
- **The bookmaker text on cards** no longer spills on 320–430px phones (probed at 7 widths).
- **Bet buttons** use full bookmaker names and say "Open" where the bookmaker only links to its AFL page.
- **Smaller items:** the comparison table names a source that's down, and "thin" says why. Tabs have proper tab semantics, and the About sheet takes keyboard focus.
